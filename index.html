The lease vote: who spoke for it, and who had given to the council

On Nov 9, Charlotte City Council approved a 10.5-year lease with American Airlines and other carriers, 8-2. More than 50 people signed up and 44 spoke. The room split almost evenly: 22 urged approval with no delay, and 21 (mostly airport workers and their advocates) asked for a delay or opposed the lease. The two sides weren't matched in organization or money. Most of the pro-lease speakers trace to the Charlotte Regional Business Alliance, and their donations went to members who voted yes.

The story

The testimony was largely organized through the Business Alliance. At least 13 of the 22 pro-lease speakers were Alliance staff, Alliance board members, worked for firms with Alliance board seats, or were tied to the Chamber. The Alliance held a press event on Nov 5. Its chief advocacy officer, a registered lobbyist, testified. Its board includes American Airlines' government-affairs director, and its executive committee includes City Manager Marcus Jones, whose staff negotiated the lease.

American Airlines didn't need to write checks. There is no money from the company or its PAC to any council member. Its influence ran through the Business Alliance, through its NC lobbying firm (a partner there testified as a restaurant-industry voice), and through small personal gifts from its government-affairs director to four members.

Speaker money tracked the yes votes. Ajmera, Mayfield, Mayo, Anderson, Watlington and Driggs each got $2,000 to $5,700 from speakers or their families and partners, including airport concessionaires, airport-area hoteliers and the first speaker, Larry Shaheen. The two no votes got about $600 combined.

Labor money went the other way, but not cleanly. SEIU and other unions put the most into Mazuera Arias and Johnson, the two no votes. SEIU also gave $1,000 each to Ajmera, Mayfield and Watlington, and all three voted yes. Turnout was even, 22 to 21. What differed was access: the business side had already given to six of the eight members who voted yes.

Anderson's filings are an open question. Her pre-election report, which shows a $9,375 one-day bundle from the Levine and Gorelick developer families, was filed after the 2025 election. Her 2023–24 reports were filed a year late. Most of her donors have no employer listed.

3. Takeaways

  1. Speakers split almost evenly: 22 for approval now, 21 for delay or against (44 spoke). The pro-lease side was largely organized through the Business Alliance. At least 6 of the 22 speakers were either CRBA staff or board/executive-committee members, or represented firms with seats on its board. American Airlines' own government-affairs director sits on that board, and so does the City Manager. I found no document showing AA or CRBA recruiting individual speakers.
  2. There is no direct American Airlines corporate or PAC money to council members. The airline exerts influence through CRBA, its lobbyists (Focus Public Affairs), and small personal checks from its government-affairs director to Ajmera, Graham, Mayfield and Mayo. Senator Tillis also threatened to revive a state takeover of the airport.
  3. Speaker money went only to members who voted yes, plus small amounts to Johnson. Mayfield (about $5.6k from speaker-linked donors) and Mayo (about $4.2k, part of it after her election) received the most. Mazuera Arias got nothing from these speakers.
  4. Labor money ran the other way, but not cleanly. SEIU gave the most to the two no votes ($3,200 and $1,500), but also $1,000 each to Ajmera, Mayfield and Watlington, who voted yes.
  5. These are mostly small, legal contributions, typical of local politics. They show who has access, not that votes were bought. Several members who voted yes (Owens, Graham, Mayfield) also criticized AA's treatment of workers and said state law (the 2026 budget provision) prevents the city from setting contractor wages.
